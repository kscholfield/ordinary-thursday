Travel journal

Melbourne, Australia

November 17–22, 2026

We arrived in Melbourne after a fifteen-hour flight and crossing the international date line. It was cool and overcast, and our first priority after checking into United Places was a long nap.

By the time we woke, the clouds had cleared. We walked through the Royal Botanic Gardens and over to the Shrine of Remembrance, then realized how hungry we were. Dinner at a restaurant next to the hotel, a stop for breakfast groceries and wine, and we were beginning to settle in.

United Places turned out to be a very good base. Our apartment had a separate bedroom, a kitchenette, a dishwasher, and a washer-dryer. The gardens were across the street, restaurants were downstairs, and the tram was an easy walk away. We could make breakfast, do laundry, and still step outside into a neighborhood with plenty going on.

The next morning, after an early mastermind call, Shirley and I made eggs and ham, coffee and orange juice, supplemented by pastries from the hotel. A leisurely breakfast at home, even when home is somewhere new.

We met our guide for a half-day introduction to Melbourne, driving through the surrounding area and past the sports complexes before parking and walking through the city center. There were restaurants everywhere, tables outside, street art, and narrow laneways full of people. Between walking and the trams and trains, getting around seemed easy.

Our guide was probably about our age. She belongs to a rowing club, rides her bike around town, and sails with her husband. They live on the Yarra River. Listening to her describe their life, I could see the appeal.

That evening, after lunch and another long nap, we walked the Tan around the Botanic Gardens. There were lots of people out walking and running, with others making their way to an outdoor concert. I liked the energy of the place. It made being out and active feel like a natural part of the day.

On Thursday, we explored on our own, on foot and by tram. At State Library Victoria, we visited the La Trobe Reading Room, with its enormous dome, galleries of books, and rows of desks beneath green lamps. A beautiful place to sit and read—or simply stand and look.

We also visited Rebel Heart: Love Letters and Other Declarations, where we took our photograph beneath a glowing blue heart. Then it was on to Carlton Gardens and the Royal Exhibition Building, with plenty more wandering along the way.

Friday was a quieter day, with rain in the forecast. We took a tram into the city center, then rode the number 35 City Circle tram around town. Lunch was a very good bowl of pho, followed by a shopping stop for a rain shell for Shirley and more walking before heading back.

Our final full day took us out to the Mornington Peninsula with our guide, Kaori. We started with coffee and a Vegemite scroll, then visited Moonlit Sanctuary for a closer look at some of Australia’s native animals. Koalas, kangaroos, and an emu that made it into our photograph together.

At Ten Minutes by Tractor, we tasted local wines and did some quick calculations about how many bottles we could take on our flight the following day. We chose a few favorites, stopped for a view across Port Phillip toward Melbourne, and finished with a wonderful lunch at Rare Hare. Kaori was knowledgeable, engaging, and easy to spend the day with.

We left Melbourne with a strong sense that we would enjoy returning. There is plenty to visit, but what appealed to me most was how easily we could imagine an ordinary routine here: breakfast in the apartment, a walk through the gardens, a tram into town, and somewhere good to eat nearby.

We’ve been discovering that some places make us want to see more, while others make us want to stay longer. Melbourne did both.
