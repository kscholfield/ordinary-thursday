Hawaiʻi — A Place That Felt Like Home

November 2–14, 2026Westin Hapuna Beach Resort · Island of Hawaiʻi

After Peru and a quick stop in Southern California, we arrived on the Big Island ready to slow down. Our room at the Westin Hapuna wasn’t ready, so we walked the property, had a drink, and began settling into a different pace. By dinner, we were watching the sun disappear into the Pacific. We were asleep before eight.

The next morning, I went to the gym for the first time in ages. Walking back to our room, I had the distinct feeling that I was home. It surprised me how quickly that feeling arrived.

Shirley’s version came when we walked down to the beach. She stepped into the warm water and announced that she didn’t think we would find her inside much that week. We’ve enjoyed beautiful coastlines on our travels, but warm, comfortable water makes a difference. Here, getting in was easy. Getting out was less appealing.

For much of the first week, our days were simple: breakfast, the beach, a swim, sometimes a nap, and dinner. There was very little we felt we needed to accomplish. We talked about whether we might eventually buy a place on Maui, but Shirley pointed out that there were still too many places she wanted to see. For now, returning to Hawaiʻi might be enough.

Our biggest adventure began with a visit to the concierge on Thursday morning. We had gone upstairs to ask about plans for Saturday. Instead, she told us that Kīlauea was erupting, that she had a rental car available, and that we could make the drive that day.

We rented the car, persuaded a friend to join us, and headed for Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park.

There were plenty of other people with the same idea, but the rangers kept traffic moving, and we found a place to park. After a short walk, we were looking across the crater at a spectacular fountain of lava. Bright orange molten rock rose into the air and flowed across the dark ground beneath a towering plume. The photographs capture some of it. Standing there was something else.

The following day, we stopped by to thank the concierge. She told us the eruption had ended around six that evening, about an hour after we had left. We had been very lucky.

Later, Shirley said that in our earlier life we probably wouldn’t have made the drive. We would have been working on something for the business or getting ready for the event. That stayed with me. Having the freedom to change the day’s plans—and actually doing it—is part of what we hoped this chapter would allow.

As friends arrived for the Business Success Summit, the pace changed again. There were conversations by the pool, dinner together, and familiar faces we were happy to see. The event has been part of our lives for years. Our relationship to the business content has changed, but the friendships still matter. It was good to reconnect with people who knew us during a very different season of life.

We signed up to return next year and began talking about spending longer in Hawaiʻi around the event. A month here sounds increasingly appealing.

When we left on November 14, we were sad to go—even with four months in Australia and New Zealand ahead of us. We keep discovering places we could happily stay longer. Hapuna was one of them.

I’ve said more than once that I hope I never get numb to this life. Watching an eruption, swimming with Shirley, or sitting down with old friends, I kept coming back to the same thought: I can’t believe this is our life.
