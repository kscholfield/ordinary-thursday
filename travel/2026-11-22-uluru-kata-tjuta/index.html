Uluṟu & Kata Tjuṯa, Australia

November 22–25, 2026

We arrived at Longitude 131° after an early flight from Melbourne. It was hot, and we were ready for breakfast, a little time to settle in, and eventually a nap. Beyond the lodge, Uluṟu rose from the surrounding plain. We had three nights here, and by the end we wished we had allowed another day or two.

Our first afternoon included a drive around much of Uluṟu and a short walk at its base. Our guide shared stories about the landscape and explained that some places were sacred and could not be photographed. There was much more to understand here than the familiar outline of the rock.

The next morning began before sunrise, with photographs from our patio during the blue hour. Later, after a guided introduction, we walked about seven kilometres around the base of Uluṟu. It was already around 86°F by the time we finished. Up close, the scale, contours, and changing surfaces gave us plenty to look at along the way.

That evening brought drinks and canapés at a sunset viewing area, followed by a walk through Field of Light. Afterward, we had dinner outside under the stars at Table 131°. When we returned to our room, the staff had prepared the patio bed with a sleeping bag, lit the fire, and set out after-dinner drinks. We slept outside under the stars. It was beautiful.

On our last full day, we visited Kata Tjuṯa and walked into Waḻpa Gorge, between its enormous domes. The amount of greenery surprised us. We enjoyed the walk and learning how these very different formations came about, then stopped at the park’s Cultural Centre to browse and have a coffee.

Sitting at a picnic table, Shirley mentioned how much she liked being here, though she couldn’t quite put her finger on why. Perhaps it was the serenity, the expansiveness, the smell of the air, or the unspoiled landscape. I felt much the same.

Longitude 131° was a wonderful base. The accommodation, food, and service were excellent. Our only wish was for more time at a slower pace. On the guided walks, we sometimes felt ready to linger when it was time to move on. Left to ourselves, we would have wandered longer or found somewhere to sit and take it all in.

Our departure morning brought a spectacular sunrise. Then it was time to pack and leave for the next part of the trip. Even knowing another great place was ahead, we were sorry to go.
