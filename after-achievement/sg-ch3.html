The Second Game

Chapter 3

Drift Is Incremental

Keith Scholfield

Drift Rarely Announces Itself

Drift does not arrive as a dramatic failure.

It arrives quietly.

A small adjustment. A minor exception. A reasonable decision.

Each one makes sense on its own.

That is why drift is difficult to recognize while it is happening.

Life still feels full. Experiences are still enjoyable. Nothing feels broken.

Which is exactly why it continues.

The Environment of Indulgence

Certain environments make drift easier.

Travel is one of them.

Indulgence becomes the default.

Meals are prepared for you. Schedules loosen. Effort becomes optional.

None of these are problems individually.

But together, they create a pattern.

Drift rarely comes from one decision. It comes from many small ones.

Comfort Compounds

Comfort has momentum.

The easier something becomes, the more natural it feels to repeat it.

Over time, the path of least resistance becomes the default.

Small indulgences become habits. Habits reshape expectations.

Eventually, what once felt like a break from discipline becomes normal.

The Narrowing of Appetite

One of the quietest consequences of drift is the narrowing of appetite.

Few people consciously decide to want less from life.

Instead, the appetite for challenge fades gradually.

A slightly easier choice. A slightly less demanding option. A slightly reduced effort.

Each one feels harmless.

Over time, they compound.

What once felt exciting begins to feel unnecessary. What once felt energizing begins to feel inconvenient.

Appetite often fades before ability.

Recognizing the Signals

The signals were there earlier than I realized.

The shirt. The fading discipline. The sense of being slightly outside the arenas that once defined me.

None of these were dramatic.

But together, they pointed to something important.

Without structure, freedom drifts.

Recognizing drift early is one of the most important skills in the second game.

Not to eliminate it entirely—but to shorten its duration.

Because the earlier drift is recognized, the easier it is to correct.

And the more runway remains for what matters.

Drift is normal.

The risk is how long it goes unnoticed.
