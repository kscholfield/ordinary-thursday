The Second Game

Chapter 5

Sequence Perishable Experiences

Keith Scholfield

The Illusion of Endless Time

One of the most common assumptions after achievement is that time expands.

And in one sense, it does.

Schedules open. Obligations recede. The calendar becomes flexible.

But not all time is equally usable.

Some experiences are perishable.

The Nature of Perishable Experiences

Certain experiences require a specific combination of health, energy, curiosity, willingness, and momentum.

When those align, the experience is accessible.

When they fade, the opportunity quietly disappears.

This does not mean life becomes smaller with age. In many ways, it becomes richer.

But the type of experiences available changes.

Some require capacity that is not permanent.

Designing the Sequence

This realization changed how we approached planning.

At first, everything felt equally available.

Over time, we began to see that sequence matters.

Some experiences require physical effort—remote environments, demanding terrain, sustained movement.

Others can be enjoyed later—cities, culture, slower travel, time with family.

If we wanted to pursue physically demanding environments, the early years were likely the best window.

So we adjusted.

More demanding experiences moved forward. Less demanding ones could wait.

The Sailing Couple

One evening, we met a couple who had spent decades sailing each summer through Europe.

They weren't rushing. They weren't optimizing.

They had simply chosen to live those years while the window was open.

Not postponed. Not deferred.

Lived.

Curveballs Are Not Interruptions

Life does not unfold cleanly.

Health changes. Family needs arise. Plans shift.

In our own lives, this became clear through an unexpected diagnosis.

It disrupted everything.

And clarified everything.

The future is not guaranteed. It never was.

Which is why sequencing matters.

Not because life can be controlled—but because waiting has a cost.

The interruption is not separate from life.

It is life.

Later Seasons

Not all meaningful experiences require physical effort.

Later seasons often emphasize stillness, relationships, reflection, and contribution.

These are not lesser experiences.

They are different ones.

The goal is not to do everything.

The goal is to do the right things while the window is open.
